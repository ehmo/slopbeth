# Density and unsummarizability

The target standard: make the writing hard to summarize because removing words removes interesting ideas, not because the prose is obscure.

## Sentence-load test

Each sentence should add at least one of:

- claim
- example
- constraint
- image
- number
- named entity
- decision
- tradeoff
- mechanism
- consequence
- contradiction
- change in the author's stance

If deleting a sentence only removes rhythm or a transition, cut or merge it.

## Summary-loss test

Compress the passage by half. A strong passage should lose specific ideas quickly. A weak passage can shrink without losing much because it was mostly scaffolding.

Failures:

- fewer words but the same generic thesis
- shorter prose that drops the evidence
- sentences that sound sharp but could apply to any topic
- every paragraph ending with an aphorism

## Topic-swap test

Replace the key nouns with a different domain. If the paragraph still mostly works, it is too generic. Add the missing mechanism, concrete stake, or observed detail.

## Dense does not mean cramped

Density is not maximum compression. Keep enough rhythm for the reader to understand the claim on the first pass. A sentence can be longer when it carries structure, contrast, and evidence.

## Rewrite moves

- Replace a vague benefit with a before/after capability.
- Replace a summary opener with the most consequential fact.
- Replace "important" with the actual cost of ignoring it.
- Replace category words with names, numbers, scenes, or mechanisms.
- Keep productive friction: doubt, specificity, and tradeoffs make prose less interchangeable.
